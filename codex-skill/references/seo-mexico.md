# TikTok Shop Mexico SEO

Use for new Mexico Listings, substantive title/keyword/positioning revisions, keyword research or SEO audits. Follow [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) for scope, market, language and output rules; narrow corrections do not restart the full research workflow. For another market, use its own language and sources rather than the Mexico examples below.

## SEO Goal

Optimize for relevant Mexican buyer searches and conversion-qualified traffic, not maximum keyword count. Platform compliance and buyer-search optimization are separate checks: correct length and formatting alone do not establish an optimized Listing. Product accuracy and intent match outweigh raw repetition. Treat the title, category, attributes, description, variants, and any available search-keyword field as one semantic system. Apply this work during ordinary Listing generation without expanding the default deliverable into a report.

## Evidence and Research

Start with the verified product type, specifications, use cases, and included contents. Build a small set of Spanish seed queries, then use available browsing, connected tools, or supplied shop data for a focused search-evidence pass before drafting. Do not stop at reading platform title policies. If the user prohibits browsing, or useful sources require unavailable access, finish with relevant inferred candidates rather than blocking the Listing or repeatedly retrying inaccessible sources.

Prefer evidence in this order, accounting for recency and product relevance:

1. The user's Mexico shop search-query and conversion data, or current Seller Center keyword recommendations with their supplied metrics.
2. Actual TikTok Shop Mexico search autocomplete, related searches, and product-search evidence.
3. Relevant Mexico listings and category terminology as vocabulary evidence; call listings high-performing only when performance data supports that claim.
4. TikTok Creative Center or other TikTok trend evidence, distinguishing content/video/hashtag interest from product-purchase searches.
5. Google Trends, Google autocomplete, or other Mexico marketplace searches as supporting evidence only; never relabel them as TikTok Shop dropdown results.

Record each retained term's source, market, observation date, search surface, seed query where relevant, and available metric internally. A public web result containing a phrase is not proof that it appears in TikTok autocomplete. Direct observation or a supplied screenshot/export is needed to call a phrase an observed dropdown term; note when the market or observation date is unknown. Autocomplete is an observed suggestion, not proof of search volume, conversion, stable ranking, or universal availability.

Distinguish **observed search terms / 已验证搜索词** from **inferred candidates / 推导候选词**. State what was verified: appearance in a specific source does not verify popularity or effectiveness. Never invent search volume, competition, ranking, trend status, or performance uplift. If a keyword analysis is requested and live evidence is unavailable, label it `基于商品相关性与墨西哥本地表达推导，未验证实时搜索量`. Routine Listings need no evidence table; disclose the limitation briefly when the user specifically requested live, popular, or dropdown keywords that could not be verified.

For a local wording or formatting correction, retain relevant existing keyword choices unless the requested change affects search intent or factual accuracy. Do not repeat research solely because a sentence changed.

Do not blindly copy competitor titles. Exclude competitor brands, misleading compatibility terms, irrelevant traffic words, and claims unsupported by the product.

## Keyword Map

Build only terms supported by the product:

- **Primary keyword:** the clearest high-intent product phrase a Mexican buyer would search, normally one main phrase.
- **Secondary keywords:** close product synonyms and supported local terms for the same product.
- **Specification and feature terms:** verified type, power source, size, capacity, function, or included accessories; use them to qualify the purchase intent.
- **Long-tail keywords:** product + verified specification + application, audience, material, or kit contents.
- **Local synonyms:** genuine Mexican variants that broaden matching without changing the product type.
- **Autocomplete and related-search terms:** retain only actually observed suggestions that fit the product; these are evidence sources and may also be core or long-tail terms, not a mandatory extra set of words to insert.
- **Excluded terms:** tempting but inaccurate, trademarked, unsupported, overly broad, or wrong-intent queries.

Filter out terms requiring unsupported claims before comparing demand. Then prioritize product relevance, buying intent, available Mexico search/conversion evidence, and readability. Map each retained keyword to a listing field and avoid forcing every term into the title. If metrics are absent, explain the choice through semantic relevance rather than claiming one synonym has higher search volume.

## Field Strategy

### Title

- Put the exact primary keyword as early as naturally possible.
- Select the one or two verified details most useful to the buying decision: power source, voltage/power, size/capacity, subtype, specific task or included kit. Make the title attractive through this concrete relevance, not by listing every specification. Include supported brand/model when useful for identification.
- A flexible structure is primary product phrase + key specification/type + application + brand + useful included accessory. Adapt the order to natural Spanish and current market rules; no fixed position or character count guarantees search ranking.
- Keep the product and most useful differentiator readable near the beginning for product-card browsing. Do not invent a fixed truncation length. Avoid shortening away useful search intent merely to hit a generic 40–60-character target, or lengthening the title just to fill the available space.
- Verify current Mexico official requirements when making policy claims; do not substitute another market's rules. Distinguish platform limits, user limits, and editorial preferences. Use standard unit abbreviations in the title when required; a spelled-out equivalent may appear naturally in the description without repeating both solely for keyword coverage.
- Prefer one clear phrase for the product type. Add a synonym only if it represents a distinct common search intent and remains readable.
- Do not repeat singular/plural forms, reorder the same words, or stack synonyms merely to increase density.
- Do not add seller adjectives such as `el mejor`, `superpotente`, `premium`, `profesional`, or `industrial` unless evidence and policy allow the claim.

### Category and Attributes

Choose the most specific accurate category and complete verified attributes because structured fields can affect discovery and filtering. Never sacrifice category accuracy for perceived search volume. Mark suggested platform values for confirmation when the current category taxonomy is unavailable.

### Selling Points and Description

Follow SKILL.md's sales-copy structure: a concrete supported use, scannable benefits, concentrated specifications, verified contents and relevant fit/safety notes. Use the primary product phrase naturally near the opening, without forcing repetition between a scene heading and the first sentence. Distribute secondary and long-tail terms where they directly explain a verified benefit, specification, use case, compatibility, or package component. For example, a supported use such as `para cambiar llantas` can belong in a benefit sentence rather than being forced into a crowded title. Write for humans first and vary sentence structure naturally. Do not use hidden keyword blocks, comma-stuffed paragraphs, unrelated query lists, or duplicated title text.

### Variants

Use concise, searchable, factual variation names such as verified size, capacity, color, battery count, or kit composition. Do not turn variant labels into promotional titles, and ensure each label matches the SKU.

### Search Keyword Field

If an actual backend/search-term field exists, use relevant terms not already overrepresented, including legitimate Mexican synonyms and long tails. Do not claim that a field exists when the user's TikTok Shop template does not contain it. A requested keyword/tag list is a planning deliverable unless an input field is confirmed; hashtags are not a substitute for product-title and description optimization.

## Hardware Search Intent

For tools, buyer intent commonly centers on the exact tool type plus verified specifications and application. Candidate dimensions include:

- Power source: `inalámbrico`, `a batería`, `eléctrico`.
- Verified specification: voltage, N·m torque, W power, bar length, ton capacity, lift height, pad/belt size, speed, battery count/capacity.
- Tool subtype: `atornillador`, `percutor`, `de patín`, `de botella`, `orbital`, `roto-orbital`, `de banda`.
- Application: wood, metal, automotive workshop, paint, mortar, sanding, pruning, according to verified suitability.
- Offer composition: `con batería y cargador`, `con 2 baterías`, `con estuche`, or accessories, only when included.

Do not use a high-volume adjacent term when the product does not satisfy that intent—for example, `rotomartillo` for an ordinary drill, `motosierra profesional` without evidence, or `gato 5 toneladas` based on vehicle weight rather than rated capacity.

## SEO Deliverable and Audit

Follow SKILL.md's compact output rule for routine generation; surface keyword analysis only when requested. For a requested SEO report or search-optimization explanation, use a compact table of keyword, role, evidence/source, and placement. Distinguish observed search evidence from terms inferred from product relevance and Mexican Spanish. Leave unobserved autocomplete terms unverified rather than filling every keyword category for appearance.

For an SEO audit, report:

- Missing or weak primary keyword.
- Incorrect or ambiguous product terminology.
- Valuable verified modifiers absent from title or structured attributes.
- Repetition, stuffing, cannibalizing synonyms, irrelevant traffic terms, or unsupported modifiers.
- Title-to-category, attribute, variant, and description consistency.
- Data gaps that prevent evidence-based keyword prioritization.

Do not promise ranking, traffic, or sales results or call an untested title optimal. When analytics are later supplied, compare search impressions, product-card click-through rate, product-page conversion, and query-level results over comparable periods. Account for changes in price, images, promotions, and inventory before attributing performance to a title change; revise using evidence rather than repeatedly swapping synonyms.
